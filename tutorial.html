The SII data model

In radio interferometry, especially in the context of aperture synthesis, each pair of antennas at a given moment acquires a noisy complex measurement called visibility. This visibility corresponds to a Fourier component of the desired radio emission. The ensemble of these measurements, accumulated over the entire observation period, forms what is known as the Fourier sampling pattern. This pattern represents an incomplete coverage of the 2-dimensional (2D) Fourier plane. To better understand the imaging process, consider the following model:

Let \({\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\) denote the unknown radio image of interest. The observed radio interferometric data, \({\boldsymbol{y}} \in \mathbb{C}^M\), can be mathematically represented as: \[ {\boldsymbol{y}} = {\boldsymbol{\Phi} {\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}} + \boldsymbol{n}} \]

where:

\({\boldsymbol{n}} \in \mathbb{C}^M\) is the complex Gaussian random noise with a standard deviation τ>0 and a mean of 0.

\({\boldsymbol{\Phi}}\colon \mathbb{R}^N \to \mathbb{C}^M\) is the measurement operator that encapsulates the acquisition process via non-uniform Fourier sampling.

In this context, we use the TorchKbNufft library as the non-uniform Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) backend to implement this operator.
